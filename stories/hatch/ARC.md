# HATCH: the series arc

The spine every episode hangs on. Viewers vote on what happens next, but the votes steer *how* and *who*, never the destination. The story truth is the game's `docs/STORY.md`; this file maps it onto episodes.

## The truth (never said out loud)

- The "dust storm" is **the ash**: a living grey particulate. The people it takes become **ash-walkers**. They don't die; they slow, grey over, and keep walking. They remember a little: how to walk a street, how to stand at a door, **how to knock**.
- **The bombs are the cure.** A government program, **Lighthouse**, burns each region out from the air when the ash takes hold. Day 1 was the cities. It never quite works, so the ash comes back and each zone gets struck again.
- **Lighthouse keeps survivors alive between strikes** with tapes played by a chain of radio relays. The 6:14 broadcast is the first tape.
- **Arks:** a shelter that welds its vents shut and runs on closed-loop air (a CO2 scrubber) can sit out a strike.
- **Him:** years ago he volunteered for civil defence and read "drill" scripts into a microphone. The drills were the plan. **The voice on the tapes is his.** He built his shelter from the program's own floor plan, because he'd seen it.

## The ending (the destination)

He finds a reel labelled **Day 1, 06:14**, plays it, and hears his own voice. He understands the drills. He climbs down into the next hatch, pulls it shut, and the first line comes back: *"The hatch is sealed. The air is still. Better start cranking."* It isn't a time loop. It's a man choosing to keep doing the work, knowing exactly what it is.

The series earns that line by planting it early. Part 2 lands its rescue on "The hatch is sealed. The air is still," and the finale closes on the same words.

## How the votes work

- **Every option has to lead back to a canon checkpoint within one or two parts.** Votes decide who's with him, what it costs, and what he learns first, not whether the story happens.
- **Good votes:** two physical actions that pull different instincts (protect, escape, find out). Open or stay quiet. Go back or seal it. Take the dog or leave it with them.
- **Never put the spine to a vote:** opening the vents, telling viewers about Lighthouse, leaving the shelter for good.
- **Log every result** in `VOTES.md` (vote, split, what it changed), so later parts stay consistent with what viewers chose.
- **Open each part on the result** ("Most of you said seal it.") and pay it off visibly. A vote that changes nothing kills the next vote.

## Season 2: Shelter No. 14

A council shelter under a school. The hatch was open, from the inside. Forty names painted on a duty roster, crossed out week by week, until one name runs down to the floor: MARGARET. The broadcast now relays **his own message** from the tower. Huddles (crowds the ash fused together). "The strikes do not stop them. They still remember how to knock." Ends with a second Ark.

## Season 3: The Relay

The program's own bunker. Grey steel, red night light, zone maps with strike dates already printed on them. Operators' logs that stop mid-sentence: *"The tapes. I know that voice. I went back through the drill rosters and"*. "If you are hearing this, you have done this before." The tape room. The reel. His voice. The last line.

## Running threads

- **The static.** The 6:14 is a tape on a loop (word for word, Part 3). Per the game (`src/config/radio.ts`), static also carries other things: survivors' chatter (traders, scavenger tips, weather) and eerie bits ("Three slow, even knocks. Then static." used in Part 4). Better reception later means more of the same tape, never new words. Plan: trader chatter in Part 6 to set up Part 7's trader.
- **The Hendersons.** They opened their door (Part 3) and the hooded figure stands on their porch (Part 4). In the game, the answering machine you find in a house greets you as the Hendersons'. If he searches their house, that's what he finds.
- **The knock code.** Two knocks, then three (Part 4). Pay it off later: the right code from the wrong thing would be the series' worst knock. Use it once, near the end of season 1.
- **Milo knows the living.** Game canon: he wags at people and growls at ash-walkers. Use it as a quiet tell, never as proof.
- **The voice.** The radio, and the Part 1 alert, are his own voice through a filter. Nobody should be able to say why it feels familiar until season 3. Let comments guess.
- **The pets.** Milo (sandy mutt, one ear up) and Zoey (black cat, white patches) are the emotional anchor of season 1, and the game's two mutters about them are the rails.
- **The crank.** "Better start cranking" is his catchphrase. Use it once an episode at most.
- **Knocks.** Every knock is a cliffhanger, and most are people. The fear is that some aren't.
- **The game.** Every part ends on the end card. Things in the series (the crank, the radio at 6:14, the knock camera, the bicycle on the crank) are things you can do in the game.
